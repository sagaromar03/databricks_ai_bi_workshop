Whose data is this?

Before you dig into the tables, one question worth answering: who does this data belong to?

Short answer: it belongs to the whole market — many energy companies buying and selling from each other — not to any single company.
🏪
Picture a big marketplace where energy companies trade with each other all day. This data is the marketplace’s own logbook — every line is simply “one company sold energy to another company”. It is not one company’s private records.

How can you tell? Try it.

Pick a company. Watch how it sometimes sells and sometimes buys — so it can’t be the one company that owns the data.

The takeaway: the company you picked sells in some deals and buys in others. If this were one company’s own records, that company would be in every row and always on the same side. Instead every company shows up on both sides — the mark of a whole-market record (the kind an exchange or a regulator keeps).
