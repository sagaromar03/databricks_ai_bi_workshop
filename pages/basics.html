Energy trading — the basics

New to energy markets? Here is the handful of words you’ll meet in this dataset, in plain language. Two minutes here and the tables will make sense.

👥 Who’s who — the players

Different companies are in the market for different reasons. Knowing the type tells you why they trade.

Producer

Makes or extracts energy and sells it. Mostly a seller.

e.g. Equinor pumps gas & oil · Kong Corporation ships LNG
Utility

Delivers power to homes and businesses, so it needs to buy a lot of energy.

e.g. RWE
Trader / trading house

Doesn’t produce or use energy — it buys and sells to profit from price moves. On both sides constantly.

e.g. Vitol, Gunvor
Exchange — a place, not a trader

The marketplace itself, where everyone else meets to trade. Like a stock exchange, but for power and gas. It doesn’t take a position of its own.

e.g. Nord Pool, EEX

🤝 What’s a “counterparty”?

Every trade has two companies in it. Whoever is on the other side of your deal is your counterparty. That’s why each row in the data names two parties — and why the same company can be a counterparty on one deal and the initiator on the next.

📍 Where deals happen — exchange vs OTC

On an exchange

Trade through a central marketplace. Standardised and often anonymous — you may not know who’s on the other side.

like buying a share on a stock exchange
OTC (over-the-counter)

Two companies agree a deal directly with each other, off the exchange.

like buying a car privately instead of at an auction

⏱️ When the energy is delivered — spot vs futures

Spot / day-ahead

For delivery now or tomorrow. The price you pay today.

like buying groceries for tonight
Forward / futures

Agree a price now for delivery weeks or months later — a way to lock in a price ahead of time.

like pre-ordering next season’s harvest at today’s price

⚡ What’s actually traded — the commodities

A handful of energy products show up throughout the data:

⚡ Electricity (power) 🔥 Natural gas 🛢️ Crude oil 🚢 LNG — gas shipped as a liquid 🌍 Carbon — permits to emit CO₂

🗂️ Where you’ll see these in the tables

dim_trader the company that started the trade — the players above
dim_counterparty the company on the other side of the deal
dim_market how it traded — spot, forward, futures, on-exchange or OTC
dim_instrument what was traded — the commodity (power, gas, oil…)
dim_delivery_point where the energy is delivered (a hub or grid zone)
fact_trade the trades themselves — one row per deal, tying all of the above together
