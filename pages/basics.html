Energy trading — the basics

New to energy markets? Here is the handful of words you’ll meet in this dataset, in plain language. Two minutes here and the tables will make sense.

👥

Who’s who — the players

Different companies are in the market for different reasons. The type tells you why they trade.

🏭

Producer

mostly sells

Makes or extracts energy and sells it into the market.

Equinor pumps gas & oil · Kong Corporation ships LNG
🔌

Utility

mostly buys

Delivers power to homes and businesses, so it needs to buy a lot of energy.

RWE
💹

Trader / trading house

buys & sells

Doesn’t produce or use energy — it trades to profit from price moves. On both sides constantly.

Vitol, Gunvor
🏛️

Exchange

a place, not a trader

The marketplace itself, where everyone else meets to trade — like a stock exchange, but for power and gas. Takes no position of its own.

Nord Pool, EEX
🤝

What’s a “counterparty”?

Every trade has two companies in it. Whoever is on the other side of your deal is your counterparty.

You
Vitol
one deal
Counterparty
Shell

Swap sides and the labels swap too — so each row in the data simply lists both parties. That’s why the same company can be the counterparty on one deal and the initiator on the next.

📍

Where deals happen — exchange vs OTC

🏛️

On an exchange

Trade through a central marketplace. Standardised and often anonymous — you may not know who’s on the other side.

like buying a share on a stock exchange
🤝

OTC (over-the-counter)

Two companies agree a deal directly with each other, off the exchange.

like buying a car privately instead of at auction
⏱️

When the energy is delivered — spot vs futures

🛒

Spot / day-ahead

For delivery now or tomorrow, at today’s price.

like buying groceries for tonight
📅

Forward / futures

Agree a price now for delivery weeks or months later — a way to lock in a price ahead of time.

like pre-ordering next season’s harvest at today’s price
⚡

Where you’ll see these in the tables

Each concept above maps to a table in the dataset.

dim_traderthe company that started the trade — the players above
dim_counterpartythe company on the other side of the deal
dim_markethow it traded — spot, forward, futures, on-exchange or OTC
dim_instrumentwhat was traded — the commodity (power, gas, oil…)
dim_delivery_pointwhere the energy is delivered (a hub or grid zone)
fact_tradethe trades themselves — one row per deal, tying it all together
